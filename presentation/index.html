₹ AI course project · reinforcement learning

Teaching an AI to play an
Indian property-trading game

DQN · PPO · MaskablePPO vs rule-based bots, with an explainable web game UI

Team: names to be added

We built an original Indian-themed board game following Monopoly's rules, trained RL agents to play it, ran controlled experiments, and built a web game where you can watch or play against the AI and see why it decides what it decides.
01 · Problem

Why is this game hard for RL?

  • Chance + strategy: dice and cards decide where you land; you decide what to buy and when to build.
  • Delayed payoff: a purchase costs cash now and earns rent many turns later.
  • Long games: 100+ rounds; a win or loss signal only at the end.
  • Huge action space in the full game: 2,922 actions with trading (Bonjour, Haliem et al.).

Our question

In a 2-player game without trading, can an RL agent learn to beat a tuned rule-based player? And how much do environment design, action masking and reward shaping matter?

Frame the research question. Emphasise that we measure the effect of design choices, not just one win rate.
02 · Literature

Three papers, one arc

2014

Bailis, Fachantidis, Vlahavas

Monopoly as an MDP. Q(λ) + neural network; actions per group: spend / sell / nothing. 69.4% of wins in a 3-player table with fixed-policy and random agents.

2019

Arun et al. (TENCON, India)

Buy / do-nothing only, a feed-forward network with experience replay. A richer state representation helped.

2022

Bonjour, Haliem et al. (IEEE TETCI)

Full 4-player game with trading; 240-dim state, 2,922 actions, action masking. Weighted net-worth reward. Hybrid PPO: 91.65% vs fixed-policy agents.

We took the reduced scope of 2014/2019 and the net-worth reward + masking of 2022. We checked each paper's claims against the papers themselves.

Mention we corrected an AI-generated blueprint: the 69.4% was a 3-player result, Bailis used Q(lambda) not DQN, and the 2022 paper's first author is Bonjour.
03 · The environment

An original Indian board, Monopoly's rules

  • 40 spaces: 22 cities (Guwahati ₹60 … Mumbai ₹400) in 8 colour groups, 4 railway stations, 2 utilities.
  • ₹1,500 start, ₹200 for passing GO, classic rent tables, houses → hotel.
  • Jail (doubles or ₹50), two original 16-card decks (“Diwali bonus!”).
  • Forced liquidation → bankruptcy; 200-round limit, then net worth decides.
  • Not included: trading, auctions (as in Bailis / Arun).
  • Pure-Python engine: ~24,000 games/min; 42 tests, including invariant checks over 3,000 random games.
Original name, art and card text; no Hasbro/Funskool branding. The engine is a generator-driven state machine used by training, the API and human play.
04 · MDP design

Two ways to pose the problem

Env A · the original blueprint

  • State: 32 numbers (positions, cash, owner code per property)
  • Actions: Pass / Buy / Build, asked after every move
  • Impossible actions silently become Pass

Env B · our design

  • State: 133 numbers (owner, houses, mortgage per property, plus decision context)
  • Actions: Pass / Buy / Build on 1 of 8 groups
  • Asked only at real decisions; illegal actions masked (MaskablePPO)

The opponent bot, dice and jail rule are part of the environment. The agent's seat is fixed, and who moves first is random in every game.

This is our main experimental factor (E2).
05 · Reward

Reward the change, not the level

nw    = cash + Σ (price − mortgage) · b + houses · house_cost      b = 1.5, or 2.0 in a full colour set
share = nw_agent / (nw_agent + nw_opponent)

blueprint:  r_t = share_t                       ← always positive: pays the agent to make games longer
ours:       r_t = share_t − share_(t−1)         ← potential-based shaping (Ng et al. 1999)
            + 10 for a win / −10 for a loss

Weighted net worth comes from Bonjour, Haliem et al. (Eq. 2–3). We also tested absolute share, sparse win/loss only and no property bonus (b = 1).

The prediction: absolute share rewards survival time. The results confirm it: 139 vs 109 rounds and half the win rate.
06 · Setup

Agents, baselines, evaluation

Agents

DQN (value-based), PPO and MaskablePPO (policy gradient) from Stable-Baselines3; MLP [128, 128]; 300k steps (E6: 1M, [256, 256]).

Opponents

Random: buys 50% of the time. Fixed-Policy: spends only above ₹350 (Bailis 2014). Greedy: always buys and builds.

Evaluation

3 seeds × 1,000 games per opponent, random first player, 95% Wilson confidence intervals. 10 configurations, 30 training runs.

Baselines: Fixed beats Random 74.1%, Greedy beats Random 72.7%, Greedy vs Fixed 46.7%.

08 · Findings

What we learned

  • Design beats algorithm: decision points + masking took MaskablePPO from 10% (PPO on Env A) to 47% vs Fixed.
  • No mask can mean collapse: unmasked PPO on Env B made 90% invalid choices, never bought, and won 0%.
  • The blueprint reward misleads: absolute share gave longer games (139 vs 109 rounds) and half the win rate.
  • Robust plateau: sparse reward, no bonus, mixed opponents and 3× longer training all land at 46–47% vs Fixed.
  • The agent learned "Greedy": it buys 84–96% of offers in every group, so it matches the Greedy bot (46.7% vs Fixed).
  • Without trading or auctions, there is little room left to out-play a sensible threshold rule. Much of the strategy lives in the actions this scope removes.
Be honest: we did not beat the Fixed bot. The contribution is a controlled study showing which design choices matter, plus the plateau analysis.

11 · Wrap-up

Limitations & future work

  • 2 players, no trading or auctions; jail decided by a fixed rule (hybrid idea).
  • Prices use the classic structure in ₹; the official Indian-edition values weren't available.
  • Only 3 seeds per configuration; agents are evaluated against bots, not against people.
  • Add trading and auctions, where the 2022 paper found the strategic depth.
  • Self-play or league training instead of fixed opponents.
  • Hybrid agent (DRL + rules) as in Bonjour, Haliem et al.
  • A human study with the Play vs AI mode.
